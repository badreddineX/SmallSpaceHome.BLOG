Small apartments have a particular relationship with cleanliness: they show mess faster than larger spaces, but they are also dramatically faster to clean once you have a system. A 60-square-metre apartment can go from "looks like a disaster" to "ready for unexpected guests" in under 15 minutes if you maintain daily habits and follow a structured weekly rotation.

This guide lays out a complete cleaning system designed specifically for small Canadian apartments, covering daily, weekly, and monthly routines with a minimal product kit.

## Daily Routine: 5 Minutes

Do these four things every day, ideally at the same time (right after dinner works well). Set a timer if it helps.

### 1. Kitchen Wipe-Down (90 seconds)

Spray the kitchen counter, stovetop, and sink with all-purpose cleaner. Wipe with a microfibre cloth.

### 2. Make the Bed (60 seconds)

In a studio apartment, your bed is visible at all times. An unmade bed makes the entire space look messy regardless of how clean everything else is.

### 3. One Load of Dishes (90 seconds)

In a small kitchen, even three or four items in the sink creates visual clutter and can attract pests.

### 4. Spot-Sweep the Entry (60 seconds)

Canadian apartments accumulate dirt, salt, gravel, and slush at the entry, especially from October through April.

## Weekly Routine: 15 Minutes Per Day, Monday Through Friday

### Monday: Kitchen Deep Clean (15 minutes)

- Clean the inside of the microwave (heat a bowl of water with lemon for 2 minutes, then wipe)
- Wipe the outside of all appliances
- Clean the sink with baking soda
- Wipe cabinet fronts, especially around handles
- Empty and replace the kitchen garbage bag
- Sweep and damp-mop the kitchen floor

### Tuesday: Bathroom (15 minutes)

- Spray and scrub the shower or tub
- Clean the toilet (bowl and exterior)
- Clean the sink and mirror (white vinegar leaves mirrors streak-free)
- Wipe the faucet and surfaces
- Sweep and mop the bathroom floor

### Wednesday: Floors (15 minutes)

- Pick up anything on the floor that does not belong there
- Sweep or vacuum all hard floors, paying attention to corners
- Damp-mop with your flat mop
- Shake out or vacuum area rugs

### Thursday: Dust and Surfaces (15 minutes)

- Dust all surfaces with a microfibre cloth: shelves, nightstand, dresser, TV stand, window sills
- Wipe light switches and door handles
- Dust the top of the fridge
- Dust baseboards in the main living area
- Wipe down your desk or workspace

### Friday: Laundry and Linen Change (15 minutes active)

- Strip the bed and put on fresh sheets
- Swap out kitchen towels and cleaning cloths
- Gather and start a load of laundry
- Fold and put away clean laundry from earlier in the week

## Monthly Routine: 30 Minutes

### Inside the Fridge (8 minutes)

Remove everything. Discard anything expired. Wipe all shelves and drawers with warm water and baking soda. Wipe door seals.

### Inside the Oven (7 minutes)

Make a paste of baking soda and water, spread on interior surfaces, let sit 15-20 minutes, wipe off. Spray stubborn spots with vinegar.

### Baseboards (5 minutes)

Run a damp microfibre cloth along all baseboards.

### Window Tracks (5 minutes)

Vacuum or brush out window tracks. Wipe with a damp cloth. Use an old toothbrush with vinegar for stubborn grime.

### Under and Behind Furniture (5 minutes)

Pull out the sofa and any large furniture. Sweep or vacuum underneath. Check for moisture issues or pest evidence.

## Canadian-Specific Cleaning Challenges

### Winter Salt Stain Removal

From November through April, Canadian apartment entries collect salt stains from de-icing products.

**Removal method:** Mix equal parts white vinegar and warm water. Mop the affected area and let the solution sit for 3 to 5 minutes, then mop again with clean water.

**Prevention:** Place a boot tray at the entry ($8 to $15 at Canadian Tire or Walmart).

### Hard Water Deposits

Hard water is common across the Canadian prairies and parts of Ontario. It leaves white, chalky mineral deposits on faucets, shower heads, and inside kettles.

**Faucets:** Soak a cloth in white vinegar and wrap it around the fixture. Leave for 30 minutes to an hour, then scrub.

**Shower heads:** Fill a plastic bag with vinegar, rubber-band it around the shower head, and leave overnight.

**Kettles:** Boil equal parts vinegar and water. Let sit for an hour, then rinse thoroughly.

### Balcony Maintenance

**Spring (April-May):** Sweep thoroughly, wipe down the railing, check the drain.

**Autumn (September-October):** Clear fallen leaves, bring in or cover items that will not survive winter.

**Winter (November-March):** Keep the balcony drain clear of ice and snow. Do not pile snow against the sliding door threshold.

## When to Ask Your Landlord for Help

- **Persistent mould** that returns despite regular cleaning — likely a ventilation issue
- **Bathroom exhaust fan that does not pull air** — report for repair
- **Severely stained flooring** present before you moved in — document and report
- **Pest issues** — landlord must arrange professional treatment in most provinces
- **Broken window seals** causing excessive condensation — building maintenance issue

Always communicate maintenance issues in writing (email) so there is a record.
