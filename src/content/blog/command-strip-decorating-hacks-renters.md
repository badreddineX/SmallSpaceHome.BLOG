Let’s be real: apartment living in Canada means you can’t just hammer, drill, or paint to your heart’s content. My own Toronto attic unit has concrete walls and a landlord who’ll deduct $200 for a single nail hole—so I’ve become weirdly expert at decorating with zero damage. After years of testing every Command strip variant from Vancouver to Halifax (yes, really), I’ve narrowed down what *actually* holds weight, looks good, and survives Canadian winters.

**TL;DR:** Command strips are the renter’s best friend—*if* you know which ones to use and where. Stick to smooth, clean surfaces, apply pressure for 30 seconds, and pull straight down when removing. I’ve tested all 15 of these in real Canadian apartments (Toronto, Montreal, and Vancouver) with no deposit deductions. Bonus: every item listed costs under $15 CAD and ships across Canada.

![A clutter-free living room corner with a floating shelf held by Command strips, potted fiddle leaf fig on floor, and layered rugs](/images/scandinavian-minimalist-furniture.jpg)

## 1. Hang Heavy Curtains Without Drilling

Tension rods crack window frames, and drill mounts leave holes. Instead, use Command™ Heavy-Duty Curtain Rod Hooks (~$7 CAD for 4 at Canadian Tire). Stick one on each side of the window frame (not the wall), then thread your rod through. I’ve held up 3ft black-out curtains in my old Montreal studio this way—no sag, even through humidity and dry winters. Key: wipe the window frame with alcohol first, wait 1 hour before hanging, and never exceed the 2.3 kg per hook limit.

## 2. Create Floating Shelves with Zero Wall Contact

No more shoddy shelves that peel paint off. My go-to? Command™ picture hanging strips (~$6 CAD for 4 at HomeSense), applied to the *back* of a lightweight shelf (IKEA’s LACK side table, $19 CAD, works great). Press each strip for 30 seconds, then press the shelf firmly against the wall. I’ve held up stacks of cookbooks this way—don’t go wider than 60 cm or heavier than 2.5 kg total. And yes, they stay put in Vancouver’s damp basements.

## 3. Frame Art Without Nails or Tape

Sticking posters with tape always leaves residue, and nails = security deposit panic. I use Command™ Clear Picture Hangers (~$8 CAD for 6 at Structube) with lightweight frames (<1.5 kg). Place the hook on the wall, the sticky tab on the frame wire, and slide together. No more crooked prints. Works on glass, tile, and painted drywall—even in my friend’s Toronto high-rise where the walls feel like sandpaper.

![A gallery wall of 4 framed prints held by Command strips, no visible hardware](/images/minimalist-apartment-pin.jpg)

## 4. Mount a Pegboard Without Screws

Pegboards are amazing for kitchens and home offices, but drilling into concrete (looking at you, Toronto pre-1960s buildings) is a no-go. I cut a 24"x36" pegboard to fit, then affix it with Command™ Large Picture Hanging Strips (~$7 CAD for 8 at IKEA Canada online). Spread the strips across the back every 15 cm. I store kitchen tools in it—no wobble, no damage. Just don’t hang your heavy cast-iron Dutch oven.

## 5. Attach a Shelf Behind Your Door

Door-back storage is the renter’s secret weapon. I used Command™ Adhesive Hooks (~$5 CAD for 10 at Simons) to hang a 10 cm deep shelf behind my bedroom door. The shelf’s back had a plastic backing I glued the strips to—no visible hardware. Now it holds hair tools, laundry baskets, and extra shoes. Works on hollow-core and solid doors alike. I keep it under 3 kg total to be safe.

## 6. Mount a TV Without Drilling (Under 32 Inches)

Yes, it’s possible—if your TV is 32" or smaller and under 12 kg. My friend in Vancouver uses the Command™ TV Mounting Kit (~$24 CAD at Amazon.ca), which includes heavy-duty strips and a low-profile bracket. It holds a 28" Sony Bravia just fine for over a year. Limitations: no ceiling mounts, avoid vibrations (skip the subwoofer under it), and never move it once installed. Best for secondary bedrooms.

## 7. Hang Plants with Invisible Hooks

Hanging pothos or trailing ivy adds life without taking floor space. I use Command™ Clear Hooks (~$6 CAD for 5 at HomeSense) inside plant saucers or with lightweight macramé hangers. Stick the hook *inside* the saucer rim, then fill with soil. Works great for 6" pots up to 1.8 kg. Just don’t overwater—leakage can weaken the adhesive over time. Great for balcony railings too (stick to the *top* edge of the rail, not the side).

## 8. Hide Cords Behind Furniture With a Cord Catch

Tangled cords look messy and attract dust bunnies. I cut a small slit in the back of my IKEA MALM bed frame and ran a fabric cord cover through it, then secured the cover’s ends with Command™ mounting strips (~$6 CAD for 4 at Structube). Now my laptop charger, lamp, and phone all tuck neatly inside. No visible wires, no damage to the frame—just a clean look in my 400 sq ft Toronto bachelor.

## 9. Display Pots on the Fridge With Magnetic Strips

Your fridge’s flat steel front is a blank canvas. I stick a 30 cm Command™ Magnetic Strip (~$9 CAD at IKEA Canada) to the side panel (not the door, which flexes), then hang small ferro-magnetic pots or cans. It holds 2–3 herb planters, spices, or cutlery. Works great for tight kitchens in Montreal walk-ups. Just avoid humid spots near the stove—moisture weakens the adhesive.

## 10. Add Crown Molding Look with Wooden Trim

Want that fancy built-in vibe without renovating? I used 2.5 cm wooden dado rail trim (~$12 CAD per 2.4m at Canadian Tire), cut to fit my ceiling corners, then glued it with Command™ Mounting Tape (~$8 CAD for 2 rolls at HomeSense). The tape’s hidden behind the trim, so no visible strips. It’s held for 14 months in my past Toronto rent, and my landlord actually asked where I got it.

## 11. Mount a Mirror Without Drilling

Full-length mirrors are essential for small-space psychology, but hanging them properly usually means anchors. Instead, use Command™ Mirror Hanging Strips (~$10 CAD for 8 at Amazon.ca). These have extra-wide adhesive pads and hold up to 4.5 kg. I used them on a 60"x18" mirror in my Vancouver bathroom—stayed put through 4 humidity-filled showers daily. Always clean the wall with isopropyl alcohol first.

## 12. Create a Gallery Wall Using Frames with Wire Backing

Avoid the “one crooked frame” effect by measuring twice and using Command™ Clear Picture Hangers. My pro tip: lay your frames on the floor first, hang them with string to test spacing, then transfer the layout to the wall using painter’s tape. Each hanger goes on the wall, the wire on the back. I grouped 5 small frames (under 1 kg each) in my Montreal basement apartment—no nails, no damage.

## 13. Hang Kitchen Utensils With a Pegboard Backing

I skipped the expensive island by mounting a small pegboard above my counter, secured with Command™ Large Picture Strips (~$7 CAD for 8 at IKEA Canada). Pegboard hooks hold spoons, whisks, and even a small knife holder. Used the Command™ Adhesive Hooks (not the removable kind) for the pegboard—stronger hold. Holds up to 3 kg comfortably, and I can remove it without leaving a trace.

## 14. Use Tension Rods + Strips for Custom Storage

Combine two renter staples: tension rods (for windows or showers) and Command™ hooks. In my kitchen, I stuck hooks to the *underside* of upper cabinets, then ran a tension rod between them. Now I hang pot lids, measuring cups, and cleaning sprays. Saves counter space and looks tidy. Cost: $18 CAD for 2 rods ($6 CAD each at Canadian Tire) + $6 CAD for hooks.

## 15. Mount a Desktop Organizer in a Studio Apartment

My favorite office hack: a 12"x10" acrylic organizer (IKEA’s FIVORIG, $14 CAD) attached to the *back* of my desk with Command™ Mounting Tape. It holds pens, USB hubs, and phone stands. The desk is laminate, so the tape grips well. No drilling, no residue—even after 10 months. I pull it off in one clean piece when moving out.

## The Command Strip Priority Order

1. **Picture hanging strips**—for art, mirrors, and frames (2.3–4.5 kg)  
2. **Heavy-duty curtain hooks**—for noise-reducing blackout curtains  
3. **Mounting tape**—for shelves, trim, and heavy backs of furniture  
4. **Large picture hanging strips**—for pegboards and light shelving  
5. **Clear hooks**—for plants, small organizers, and pantry storage  

Command strips aren’t magic—they won’t stick to rough concrete or dusty surfaces—but for 95% of Canadian apartments (concrete, drywall, smooth plaster), they’re the closest thing to renter-friendly construction glue. Test one strip first, wait 24 hours, then trust it. You’ll keep your deposit *and* your sanity.
