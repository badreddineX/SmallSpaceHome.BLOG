Remote work changed the relationship between Canadian renters and their apartments. A space designed for sleeping and relaxing now also has to accommodate an 8-hour workday — in many cases without a spare room, a study, or even a defined corner to put a desk. In a studio apartment or a one-bedroom where the living room also serves as the home office, the setup matters a great deal for both productivity and the ability to mentally switch off at the end of the day.

This guide covers compact desk options, placement strategies, and the closet-office conversion that solves the problem entirely.

---

## Desk Size: The Minimum That Works

Before choosing a desk, know your actual minimum:

**Laptop only (occasional work):** 80 × 40 cm. Fits on a small wall-mounted folding desk or a corner of a dining table.

**Laptop + external monitor (daily work):** 100 × 50 cm. The monitor needs to sit far enough from your face (50–70 cm) without the desk edge pressing into your wrists.

**Desktop with dual monitors:** 140 × 60 cm minimum. This size is difficult in a studio apartment — consider a monitor arm that mounts to the wall to reduce desk depth requirements.

**Keyboard + mouse + notebook + coffee:** Add 20 cm of width and 10 cm of depth to whatever your screen requires. A desk that fits the monitor but leaves no room for anything else creates constant frustration.

## Compact Desk Options Available in Canada

### IKEA MICKE (73 × 50 cm, ~$100)

The most widely used compact apartment desk in Canada. Built-in drawer and open storage on the right side. Fits in most apartment corners. Available in white, black, and grey. Lightweight enough to move easily.

Best for: Laptop-based work, students, side-by-side with a second MICKE for a wider setup.

Limitation: 73 cm width is narrow for a monitor — consider a monitor arm if using an external screen.

### IKEA LINNMON + ADILS or ALEX legs (~$60–$200)

The LINNMON tabletop is a simple flat surface available in 100 × 60 cm and 150 × 75 cm. Combined with ADILS legs ($5 each, need 4) it makes a minimal desk at $60–$80. Combined with an ALEX drawer unit instead of rear legs, it adds filing and storage for $150–$200.

Best for: Anyone who wants a custom-width desk without paying custom-furniture prices. The 100 × 60 cm setup is the most popular small-apartment configuration.

### IKEA TROTTEN (120 × 70 cm, ~$230)

A sit-stand desk with a manual crank mechanism. In a Canadian apartment where you sit at the desk for long hours, the ability to stand periodically is a real ergonomic improvement. The 120 × 70 cm surface is larger than most compact desks but justifies the space in a dedicated home office corner.

Best for: Full-time remote workers with space for a larger desk who want sit-stand capability without a motorized (expensive) frame.

### [Wall-mounted folding desk](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0D78FBSWK?tag=smallspace06f-20) (Amazon.ca, ~$80–$150)

A desk that folds flat against the wall when not in use. Takes up zero floor space when closed — the wall profile is 10–15 cm. Open, it provides a 60–90 cm wide surface supported by fold-down legs.

Best for: Studios or small one-bedrooms where a permanent desk footprint is not acceptable. Works well for part-time remote work or students. Less suitable for daily full-time WFH — the lack of storage and the effort to set up and put away daily becomes tiring.

Mounting: 4 screws into the wall. Drywall anchors (toggle bolts) work if there is no stud in the right position. The desk must be level — use a torpedo level before tightening.

### Corner desk (L-shaped, Amazon.ca, ~$100–$200)

An L-shaped desk puts monitors on the primary arm and reference materials, notebooks, and peripherals on the return. In a corner, an L-desk uses space that a straight desk cannot. Total footprint is typically 120 × 120 cm — larger than a straight desk but uses the dead corner space efficiently.

Best for: A dedicated home office corner in a one-bedroom apartment where a corner is available and the room can accommodate the footprint.

## The Cloffice: Converting a Closet Into a Home Office

The cloffice (closet office) is the most space-efficient home office solution in a Canadian apartment. It uses the closet — a dedicated, enclosed space — for work, then closes completely when work is done.

### Which closets work

**Minimum dimensions:** 55 cm deep × 80 cm wide × 200 cm tall. Most Canadian apartment reach-in bedroom closets meet this minimum.

**Door type:** Bifold doors (two panels that fold to the side) work best — they open fully without requiring swing clearance. Sliding doors also work. Hinged doors require swing clearance and are less convenient.

### Setting up the cloffice

**Remove the closet rod and any shelving you don't need.** Clothing from this closet moves to the bedroom wardrobe, under-bed storage, or a freestanding PAX unit elsewhere in the room.

**Add a desktop:** A shelf board at 75–80 cm height (standard desk height) across the full closet width serves as the desk surface. Use IKEA shelf brackets into studs ($5–$8 each) or a freestanding shelf unit. A solid 25 mm thick board does not flex under monitor weight.

**Add shelving above:** The space above the desk (80–180 cm height) fits 2–3 shelves for books, files, a printer, and supplies. IKEA BOAXEL or simple shelf boards on brackets work here.

**Cable management:** Drill a 35 mm hole in the desk surface near the wall for cables to pass through and down to a power bar on the floor. A rubber cable grommet ($3–$5 at Canadian Tire) finishes the hole cleanly.

**Lighting:** Closets have no window — a mounted LED strip under the shelf above the desk (IKEA ALEX or MITTLED LED strip, ~$15–$30) provides direct task lighting. A clip-on monitor light (~$25–$50 at Amazon.ca) reduces eye strain.

**Chair:** An office chair that fits in the closet width (most go 60–70 cm wide). At the end of the workday, push it in and close the doors — the home office disappears entirely.

## Desk Placement in a One-Room Apartment

Where you put the desk matters as much as which desk you choose.

**Face the wall, not the room.** A desk where you face the wall while working creates a visual boundary — you sit down, face the wall, and mentally enter work mode. A desk where you face the room means your resting space is always in your eyeline during work hours.

**Not visible from the sofa.** Placing the desk so it cannot be seen from the sofa lets the living area function as a genuine rest space. In a studio, this usually means positioning the desk behind a bookshelf divider or in an alcove.

**Near a window, but not facing it.** Natural light from the side (left or right of the monitor) reduces eye strain without creating screen glare. Facing a window creates glare on the monitor and makes your face dark on video calls.

## Desk Storage in a Small Space

A desk in a small apartment needs built-in storage — loose items have nowhere else to go.

**Monitor riser:** A [monitor riser with storage drawer](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0BN159WKY?tag=smallspace06f-20) (~$30–$60 CAD) lifts the screen to eye level and reclaims the space beneath it for a keyboard or small items.

**Pegboard above the desk:** A [pegboard wall organizer kit](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0GLN15MC3?tag=smallspace06f-20) (~$30–$50 CAD) mounts above the desk and holds accessories, headphones, cables, and sticky notes on hooks. Requires 4 screws, patchable at move-out.

**Under-desk drawer unit:** IKEA ALEX drawer unit on wheels ($120–$160) rolls under the desk and provides 5 drawers for files, office supplies, and cables.

**Desktop organizer:** A simple vertical organizer (Amazon.ca, ~$15–$25) holds notebooks, folders, and small items upright on the desk surface without spreading across it.
