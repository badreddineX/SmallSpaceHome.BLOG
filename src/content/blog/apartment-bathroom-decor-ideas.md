My first apartment bathroom in Montreal was a windowless box with a builder-beige everything: beige tile, beige tub surround, a plastic mirror cabinet, and a fluorescent tube that made everyone look ill. I couldn't paint it, couldn't touch the tile, and couldn't drill a single hole. For about a year I just didn't look at it.

Then I spent one Saturday and around $180 CAD, and it went from "the room I apologise for" to genuinely the nicest small room in the apartment. Nothing I did left a mark. When I moved out, twenty minutes of unscrewing tension rods and peeling off hooks put it back exactly as I found it.

Rental bathrooms are the hardest room to decorate because everything permanent belongs to the landlord. But almost everything that *makes* a bathroom feel styled — colour, texture, light, greenery, a bit of art — is soft, freestanding, or stick-on. Here's what actually works.

**TL;DR:** Swap the three soft things first (shower curtain, bath mat, towels) in one colour family for ~$60 CAD. Add warm light (a 2700K bulb, ~$8), one leaning or freestanding shelf, adhesive hooks rated for damp rooms, a plant, and one framed print. Skip paint — use peel-and-stick tile or wallpaper on one wall instead. A full renter-safe refresh runs $120–$250 CAD and comes apart in half an hour.

## What Should You Change First in a Rental Bathroom?

The soft goods — shower curtain, bath mat, and hand towels — in one colour family. This is the single highest-impact change and it costs almost nothing.

A rental bathroom usually comes with a clear plastic liner, no curtain, a thin builder bath mat, and whatever towels you already own in five different colours. Replacing those three things with a matching set instantly makes the room look decorated, because your eye reads "someone chose this."

- **Shower curtain**: a fabric curtain (cotton or waffle weave), $20–$45 CAD at IKEA, Simons, or Amazon.ca, plus a cheap clear liner behind it. Fabric drapes better than vinyl and doesn't stick to you.
- **Bath mat**: a cotton or tufted mat, $15–$30 CAD. Skip memory foam — it goes flat and grey fast.
- **Hand towels**: two or three in a colour that works with the curtain, $8–$15 each. You don't need to match your bath towels, just the ones on display.

Pick one colour family — sage and cream, warm terracotta and white, navy and oatmeal — and the beige tile you can't change suddenly reads as a neutral background instead of the main event.

## How Do You Add Shelves Without Drilling?

Three no-drill options, in order of how much they hold: a freestanding shelf over the toilet, a leaning ladder shelf, and a tension-rod shelf in the shower.

The wall above the toilet is the most usable empty space in any small bathroom, and you never need to touch it:

- **Over-the-toilet space saver** — a freestanding frame that straddles the tank with two or three shelves above. $40–$80 CAD at Canadian Tire, Walmart, or Amazon.ca. Bamboo or matte black both look far more expensive than they are.
- **Leaning ladder shelf** — leans against the wall at an angle, no fixing at all. $50–$100 CAD. Good if you have a bit of floor space next to the sink or door.
- **Tension corner shelf** — a spring-loaded pole with baskets that wedges floor-to-ceiling in the shower corner. $25–$45 CAD. Ends the row of bottles on the tub edge.

For anything lighter — a small plant, a rolled towel, a trailing pot — a second tension rod mounted high across the window recess or between two walls gives you a hanging bar with zero hardware.

## Can You Change the Wall Without Painting?

Yes — peel-and-stick tile or wallpaper on one wall, and it removes cleanly at move-out.

Most Canadian leases require you to return the unit in its original paint colour, which makes actual paint a hassle you pay for twice. Removable wall coverings skip that entirely:

- **Peel-and-stick tile** — vinyl tiles that mimic subway, zellige, or patterned cement tile. $2–$5 CAD per square foot at Home Depot, RONA, or Amazon.ca. Best used on the wall behind the sink or as a "backsplash" strip, not the whole room.
- **Peel-and-stick wallpaper** — one accent wall (usually the wall the toilet is on, since it has the least fixtures). $30–$70 CAD for a small bathroom wall. Choose a low-contrast pattern; busy prints feel smaller in a tight room.
- **Tile decals** — stickers sized to your existing tile grid that sit on top of the ugly tile. $20–$40 CAD for a small bathroom. The most reversible option of all.

Do one wall, not four. A single papered or tiled wall reads as intentional; the whole room done in stick-on material starts to look like a rental hack.

## What About Lighting in a Windowless Bathroom?

Change the bulb to a warm 2700K, and add one battery or plug-in light for a second layer.

That blue-white fluorescent or 5000K LED is doing more damage to the room than the beige tile. Bathrooms should feel like the warm end of a hotel, not a walk-in clinic.

- **Swap the bulb**: a 2700K "soft white" or "warm white" LED, $6–$10 CAD at any hardware store. If the fixture takes a tube, warm-white LED tube replacements exist for $15–$25.
- **Add a second source**: a small plug-in wall light, a rechargeable puck light stuck to the mirror cabinet with adhesive, or an LED candle on the shelf. $15–$40 CAD. One low, warm light near the mirror changes the whole mood.
- **Mirror**: if the landlord mirror is small or plastic-framed, lean a larger framed mirror against the wall or hang one on an adhesive hook rated for its weight. A bigger mirror bounces what light you have and makes the room feel twice the size.

## How Do You Style the Bathroom Without It Looking Cluttered?

Pick three surfaces — the shelf, the back of the toilet, the windowsill or sink corner — and put one considered thing on each. Everything else goes in a drawer or a basket.

Small bathrooms tip into cluttered faster than any other room because every product is visible. The fix is editing, not more storage:

- **One plant** — pothos, ZZ, or snake plant for a dark room; a small fern or trailing ivy if you have a window. $10–$25 CAD at a grocery store or Canadian Tire garden centre. A realistic faux plant is a fine call for a genuinely windowless room.
- **One piece of art** — a framed print, a postcard in a small frame, or a pressed leaf. Bathrooms handle humidity fine if the frame has glass. $15–$35 CAD, or print something at home.
- **One set of nice-looking daily things** — decant hand soap into a plain glass or ceramic pump ($8–$15), keep cotton buds and rounds in a small lidded jar, and hide everything else. The visible 10% is what makes the room feel styled; the other 90% belongs behind a door.

A basket on the floor or the bottom shelf catches the overflow — spare toilet rolls, the hairdryer, the cleaning spray — so the surfaces stay clear.

Here's the whole thing as a shopping list, roughly in the order to buy it:

| Cotton bath mat | $15–$30 |
| 2–3 hand towels, one colour | $25–$45 |

| Over-toilet freestanding shelf | $40–$80 |

You can do the top four rows for under $110 CAD and stop there — that alone resets the room. Everything below the bulb is the difference between "clean and tidy" and "this looks like a place I chose."
