The cabinet under the sink is one of the most wasted storage zones in a small apartment. Most people push things in until the door barely closes, then forget what is in there. The drain pipe and supply lines running through the middle make standard shelving impossible, so without purpose-built organizers the space defaults to a pile.

This guide covers both bathroom and kitchen under-sink organization with specific products available in Canada.

---

## The Core Problem: The Drain Pipe

Every under-sink cabinet has a drain pipe running vertically through the middle of the space, with supply lines on either side. A single full-width shelf cannot sit flat below the drain connection. This is why standard shelf units do not work here.

The solution is to work around the pipe rather than trying to fit storage under it:

- **Left and right of the pipe**: two independent storage zones
- **Above the pipe**: an elevated shelf on the flat section beside the drain
- **Door back**: spray bottle rack or small organizer mounted to the cabinet door interior

---

## Bathroom Under-Sink Organization

### The two-zone system

**Zone 1 (one side of the pipe):** A stackable half-width bin set. Three bins stack vertically, each holding toiletries, cleaning products, or medicines. Total height of a 3-bin stack: roughly 45–50 cm — fits most bathroom vanity cabinets.

**Zone 2 (other side of the pipe):** A pull-out drawer tray or small lazy Susan for daily-access items — face wash, extra toothpaste, cotton pads. Pull-out access means you never have to reach to the back.

**Above the pipe:** An adjustable under-sink shelf ($20–$35 at Amazon.ca) creates a surface above the drain pipe connection, adding storage for light items — extra soap bars, travel toiletry bags, small cleaning bottles.

### What to keep under the bathroom sink

- Backup toiletries (extra shampoo, soap, toothpaste — 1 backup per item maximum)
- Cleaning supplies for the bathroom only
- Medicines and first aid (in a dedicated small bin, labelled)
- Cotton pads, cotton swabs, hair ties — loose small items go in a small bin, not loose

**What does not belong here:** Hair tools (they overheat in an enclosed cabinet), makeup (humidity damages product formulas), towels (they absorb odours), and anything you need to access more than twice a day (too inconvenient).

## Kitchen Under-Sink Organization

The kitchen sink cabinet is typically wider than a bathroom vanity (60–80 cm) and deeper (55–60 cm). It also has more competition for space — trash bin, cleaning supplies, and often random overflow items that have nowhere else to go.

### Clear the cabinet first

Remove everything. Most kitchen under-sink cabinets contain at least 3–4 items that belong elsewhere (plastic bags crammed in a corner, random tools, extra food items). Clear it completely before adding any organizer.

**What actually belongs under the kitchen sink:**
- Trash bin (if no separate space)
- Recycling bag or bin
- Cleaning supplies: dish soap, spray cleaners, sponges, scrubbers, garbage bags
- Dishwasher pods (if you have a dishwasher)
- Drain cleaner, plunger

That is it. Everything else finds a better home elsewhere.

### Pull-out bin organizer

A pull-out organizer with two bins — one for trash, one for recycling — is the most practical kitchen under-sink upgrade. The bins slide out on a rail when you open the cabinet door, then push back in cleanly. This solves the trash-bin-blocking-everything problem that most kitchen sink cabinets have.

**Cost:** $40–$80 at Amazon.ca or IKEA (HALLBAR pull-out waste sorting, ~$50).

### Spray bottle rack on the door

A tension rod or a dedicated over-door rack mounted to the inside of the cabinet door holds spray bottles by their trigger handles — no floor space used. A tension rod ($8–$12, Canadian Tire) pressed between the door frame sides works perfectly: hang spray bottle handles over the rod.

### Cleaning caddy

A portable cleaning caddy (a handled bin that holds cleaning supplies) lets you grab everything at once when cleaning the bathroom or kitchen — no multiple trips to the cabinet. Store it beside one wall of the under-sink cabinet rather than in the middle where it blocks the drain.

**Cost:** $10–$25 at Canadian Tire, Walmart Canada, or Amazon.ca.

## Five-Minute Under-Sink Fix

If you want a fast, low-cost improvement without buying a full organizer system:

1. Remove everything, wipe the cabinet floor
2. Add a tension rod inside the door for spray bottles (one rod, $10)
3. Place a small lazy Susan on one side of the pipe for cleaning products ($12–$15)
4. Stack a plastic bin on the other side for backup toiletries or supplies
5. Label anything that is not immediately visible

Total cost: $25–$40. Time: 20 minutes. Result: the cabinet is usable without digging.
