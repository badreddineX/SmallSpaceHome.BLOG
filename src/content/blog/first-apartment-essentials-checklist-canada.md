Moving into a first apartment in Canada means figuring out what you actually need versus what every "essentials list" online tells you to buy. Most of those lists are written for American readers, priced in USD, and assume you have a car to haul everything home from Target — which doesn't exist here.

This checklist is specifically for Canadian renters. Real CAD prices, Canadian stores (IKEA, Canadian Tire, Amazon.ca, Dollarama, Facebook Marketplace), and a priority order that puts daily essentials before furniture and decor.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Buy in priority order: sleep → bathroom → kitchen → cleaning → furniture → decor.
> - A functional first apartment costs roughly $1,500–$2,500 CAD new, or $800–$1,400 CAD mixing secondhand.
> - Facebook Marketplace saves 40–60% on furniture in most Canadian cities.
> - Skip the coffee table, TV stand, and full dining set — add those once you know how the space works.

**TL;DR:** Sleep setup first (mattress + pillow + sheets, ~$350–$550 CAD), then bathroom basics (~$40 CAD), kitchen essentials (~$60 CAD), and cleaning supplies (~$25 CAD). That's ~$475–$675 CAD to be fully functional on day one. Furniture and decor come after.

## The Priority Order: What to Buy First

Most first-apartment mistakes happen because people buy furniture and decor before solving the basics. A sofa looks nice, but you can sit on the floor for a week — you can't sleep without a mattress or cook without a pan.

**The order that works:**
1. Sleep setup
2. Bathroom basics
3. Kitchen essentials
4. Cleaning supplies
5. Furniture (sofa, table, storage)
6. Decor and styling

Follow this sequence and you'll never be stuck in an apartment with a nice rug but no towels.

## 1. Sleep Setup (~$350–$550 CAD)

The mattress is the single most expensive purchase and the one most worth spending on. You'll spend a third of your life on it, and a bad mattress wrecks everything else — energy, mood, productivity.

| Item | Cost (CAD) | Where |
|------|-----------|-------|
| Mattress (queen, boxed) | $250–$400 | Amazon.ca, IKEA, Walmart |
| Pillow (2-pack) | $25–$40 | Amazon.ca, IKEA |
| Sheet set (queen) | $30–$50 | IKEA, Walmart |
| Duvet + cover | $50–$80 | IKEA |
| **Total** | **$355–$570** | |

**Canadian-specific tip:** IKEA Canada's VESTERÖY mattress (~$349 CAD for a queen) is the best-value boxed mattress available in-store. Amazon.ca bed-in-a-box options (Zinus, Linenspa) start around $250 CAD. Skip the bed frame for now — the mattress goes on the floor until you find a frame on [Facebook Marketplace](/blog/studio-apartment-furnishing-cost-canada) for $50–$100 CAD.

For long-term bedroom storage, a bed frame with built-in drawers (IKEA NORDLI, $399–$699 CAD) replaces the need for a dresser entirely — see our [small bedroom storage guide](/blog/small-apartment-bedroom-storage-ideas) for the full system.

## 6. Decor — Last, Not First (~$50–$200 CAD)

Decor is the fun part, but it's the last priority. An apartment with good lighting and clear surfaces looks better than one with art on every wall and clutter on every shelf.

**The only day-one decor purchase:** warm 2700K LED bulbs (~$15 CAD for a 4-pack at Canadian Tire). Swap every bulb in the apartment — it transforms how the whole space feels in five minutes.

Everything else — rugs, art, plants, cushions — can wait. When you're ready, follow the priority order in our [budget apartment decor guide](/blog/storage-ideas-for-small-places) (covers both $50 and $200 CAD tiers) or go the dollar-store route with [Dollarama finds that look expensive](/blog/first-apartment-essentials-checklist-canada).

## What to Skip Entirely (For Now)

These are the items every "essentials list" includes that first-apartment renters in Canada don't actually need on day one — or often at all:

- **Coffee table** — wait until you know your living room layout; most small apartments don't have room for one
- **TV stand** — the TV sits on the dresser, a shelf, or a $30 CAD wall mount
- **Full dining set** — a small 2-person table and two chairs is enough for a one-bedroom
- **Bookshelf** — IKEA KALLAX does double duty as storage and display for ~$79 CAD
- **Desk lamp** — the floor lamp covers the desk area too, in a small apartment
- **Matching anything** — coordinated sets look nice but cost 2–3x more than buying individual pieces that simply don't clash

## The 30-Day First Apartment Timeline

**Day 1:** Sleep setup + bathroom basics + cleaning supplies. You can sleep, shower, and clean.

**Day 2–3:** Kitchen essentials. You can cook.

**Week 1:** Swap light bulbs. Add a floor lamp. Buy hangers and unpack clothes.

**Week 2:** First furniture — a small table and chair, or a desk if you work from home (see our [small apartment home office guide](/blog/small-apartment-home-office-ideas)).

**Week 3–4:** Add the sofa and any remaining furniture. Check [Facebook Marketplace](/blog/studio-apartment-furnishing-cost-canada) before buying new.

**Month 2:** Start the [organization system](/blog/storage-ideas-for-small-places). Add decor [on a budget](/blog/storage-ideas-for-small-places).

The apartment doesn't need to be finished in a weekend. It needs to be liveable on day one, comfortable by week two, and organized by month two. Everything else is gradual.
