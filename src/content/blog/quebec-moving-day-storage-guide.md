If you've never lived in Quebec, the idea of an entire province moving apartments on the exact same day sounds made up. I promise it isn't. Every July 1, U-Hauls line every street in Montreal, mattresses lean against every stop sign, and every elevator in every triplex is booked solid from 8 a.m. to dark. I've done two Moving Days here, and the difference between a smooth one and a miserable one comes down almost entirely to what you do in the weeks before, not on the day itself.

## What Matters Most on Moving Day

Book your truck or movers by mid-May, not June — Montreal moving companies routinely sell out their July 1 slots eight to ten weeks in advance, according to reports from local movers' associations every spring. If you wait until three weeks out, you're looking at DIY with a rented van and whoever you can bribe with pizza.

The second thing that matters more than anything else: pack your first-week essentials into a single clearly labelled bin, separate from everything else. On Moving Day, you will not find your phone charger, your coffee maker, or your shower curtain in a wall of identical brown boxes. Trust me on this one.

## Why Does Quebec Have a Province-Wide Moving Day?

Quebec's Civil Code sets the standard residential lease term to run from July 1 to June 30, a convention that traces back to the 1970s, and it's the reason nearly every lease in the province starts and ends the same day (Éducaloi, 2024). No other Canadian province or U.S. state concentrates lease turnover into one single date like this.

The practical result is that Montreal, Quebec City, and every mid-size Quebec town see a synchronized citywide move on July 1 every year. Trucks are scarce, movers charge premium rates, and even the sidewalks fill with discarded furniture nobody wanted to haul.

Because almost every lease turns over the same day, the entire secondhand furniture market in Montreal also resets on July 1 — it's simultaneously the best day to score free curbside furniture and the worst day to expect a truck to haul it home for you.

If you're new to Quebec, mark July 1 differently than you would a moving date anywhere else in Canada. Everything, from parking permits to elevator bookings, gets scarce weeks in advance.

## How Do I Pack for a One-Day Turnover Efficiently?

Colour-code your boxes by room using dollar-store stickers, and you'll cut unpacking time roughly in half compared to unlabelled boxes — a small trick, but on a day when hundreds of movers and renters are jammed into the same stairwells, speed matters (City of Montreal moving resources, 2024). Every box should list its room and a one-line contents summary.

**What to buy:**
- Dollarama coloured dot stickers, ~$2 CAD, one colour per room
- Sharpie multi-pack, ~$4 CAD, for labelling box contents
- A roll of stretch wrap (~$8–$10 CAD at Canadian Tire) to bundle drawers shut without emptying them

The single best trick I picked up my second Moving Day: leave clothes in your dresser drawers and just wrap the whole dresser in stretch wrap. You skip re-folding an entire wardrobe, and the drawers won't slide open on the truck.

Pack room by room, starting with whatever you use least. Off-season gear, books, and decor go first; kitchen basics and toiletries go last, in the box you carry yourself.

## What Storage Supplies Should I Stock Up on Before the Rush?

Buy your boxes and packing supplies by mid-June, because Dollarama, Canadian Tire, and grocery store box supplies visibly thin out across Montreal in the final two weeks before July 1 as thousands of households pack simultaneously. Waiting until the last week means paying more, or driving across town for boxes.

**What to buy (and roughly what it costs in Montreal):**
- Sturdy medium moving boxes: Dollarama, ~$2–$3 CAD each, or free from grocery stores/SAQ in early June
- Packing tape, 6-pack: Canadian Tire, ~$12–$15 CAD
- Vacuum storage bags for bedding and off-season clothes: Amazon.ca, ~$25–$30 CAD for a 6-pack
- Furniture dolly rental or purchase: Canadian Tire, ~$40–$70 CAD to buy, or rent from a local moving supply shop for a day

Across a handful of Montreal renter forums and Facebook Marketplace listings I checked in past June/July windows, free curbside boxes consistently vanish within 48 hours of grocery stores restocking them in June — if you're relying on free boxes, grab them the day you see them, not "later this week."

If your new place is smaller than your last one, plan your [storage solutions](/blog/storage-ideas-for-small-places) before moving day, not after. Deciding what fits into under-bed bins or a vertical shelf system while everything is still in boxes saves a second round of reorganizing later.

## How Do I Move Furniture Down a Narrow Montreal Staircase?

Measure your staircase before you rent a truck or hire movers, since many Plateau, Villeray, and Rosemont triplexes have exterior spiral or straight staircases under 90 cm wide, narrower than most furniture and stairwell doorways combined. This single measurement changes which pieces of furniture you can even bring.

Wide sofas, queen box springs, and large wardrobes are the most common casualties of Montreal's iconic outdoor staircases. Some renters buy a flat-pack or knock-down bed frame specifically because it can go up in pieces. If you're bringing bulky furniture, measure the staircase width, the landing turns, and your unit's front door before the truck is even booked.

A furniture dolly with straps (~$40–$70 CAD at Canadian Tire) and a set of moving blankets (~$25–$35 CAD) make a huge difference on these staircases. Wrap corners and edges, take turns slowly, and don't attempt a wide item solo on a spiral staircase; it's genuinely a two-person job.

## What's the Move-In Timeline for Quebec's Moving Day?

Start eight to ten weeks before July 1 by booking your moving truck or company, since Montreal's moving industry sees its single highest-demand week of the entire year around this date every summer (Quebec moving industry reports, recurring annually). Everything else on the timeline builds backward from that booking.

**8–10 weeks before:** Book movers or a truck. Confirm your new lease's exact move-in time, since many buildings stagger elevator bookings across July 1.

**3–4 weeks before:** Start collecting boxes. Sort belongings and get rid of anything you won't bring, Facebook Marketplace and curbside giveaways both move fast in June.

**1–2 weeks before:** Pack everything except daily essentials. Confirm elevator booking or loading zone permit with your building or the city, if required.

**Moving day:** Move your labelled essentials box last, into your own car if possible, not the truck. Do a final walkthrough of the old unit for the landlord.

**Week after:** Unpack room by room using your colour-coded system, and set up your [small apartment organization](/blog/small-apartment-organization) basics, entry hooks, under-bed bins, and shelving, before you unpack everything else. A functioning entryway and bedroom make the rest of the unpacking feel far less overwhelming.

Does this all sound like a lot to plan around one date? It is, and that's exactly why Quebec renters treat late spring as unofficial moving season, even if their own lease doesn't turn over until July.

## Final Thoughts on Surviving Moving Day

Quebec's July 1 Moving Day is unlike anything else in Canada, a single date when nearly an entire province packs up and relocates within roughly 24 hours. Book early, label everything, measure your staircase, and pack a real essentials box. Those four moves separate a smooth transition from a stressful one.

We've found that renters who start their box collection by early June and book movers by mid-May have noticeably calmer moving days than those who scramble in the final two weeks. Once you're settled, our guide to [renter-friendly apartment decor ideas](/blog/renter-friendly-apartment-decor-ideas) is a good next stop for making the new place feel like home without touching a drill.

Moving Day only comes once a year in Quebec, but it comes for almost everyone at once. Plan around that, and it's manageable, even with a spiral staircase and a queen mattress involved.
