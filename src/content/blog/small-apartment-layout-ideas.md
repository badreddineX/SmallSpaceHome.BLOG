Most small apartments feel cramped because of the layout, not the size. Furniture pushed against every wall, a sofa that's too big, and a walkway that runs right through the middle of the room can make a comfortable apartment feel tight.

These small apartment layout ideas are built around the shapes Canadian renters actually live in: narrow living rooms, squarish one-bedrooms, open-plan condos and rooms with awkward windows or heaters. Most cost nothing to try, since they're about placement rather than buying anything new.

## The Numbers to Plan Around

Layout advice gets much easier once you know a few clearances. These are commonly recommended guidelines, not strict rules, and you can bend them a little in a very small room.

| Situation | Common guideline |
|---|---|
| Main walkway through a room | about 75 to 90cm (30 to 36 in) |
| Secondary paths | about 60cm (24 in) |
| Sofa to coffee table | about 35 to 45cm (14 to 18 in) |
| Beside a bed (for making it and getting in) | about 60cm (24 in) on at least one side |
| In front of drawers or a closet door | enough to fully open them and stand in front |
| Dining chair pushed back from a table | about 75 to 90cm behind the chair |

If you can't hit all of them, protect the main walkway first. That's what makes a room feel usable.

## 1. Plan on Paper or With Tape First

Before you move any furniture, measure the room and mark it out.

1. **Measure the walls, windows, doors and heaters,** including the door swing.
2. **Sketch a simple plan** on paper, or use a free online floor planner.
3. **Mark furniture on the floor** with painter's tape or cut cardboard to the same size.
4. **Walk through it.** Check doors, drawers, closet swings and the path from the entrance.

This takes about 30 minutes and saves you from carrying a sofa across the room twice.

## 2. Scale the Furniture to the Room

The single most common layout mistake is furniture that's simply too big. A full-size sofa in a small living room dominates the space before you've added anything else.

- **Choose an apartment-size sofa or a loveseat** with slim arms.
- **Pick furniture with visible legs.** Seeing the floor underneath makes a room feel larger.
- **Use a round or oval coffee table** in tight spaces, because there are no corners to catch.
- **Skip bulky armchairs** and consider a pair of lighter accent chairs or poufs that can move around.

## 3. Float the Sofa Instead of Pushing Everything Against the Walls

It sounds counter-intuitive, but pulling the sofa 10 to 20cm off the wall usually makes a room feel bigger, not smaller, and gives you space behind it for a console table or a lamp. In a longer room, a sofa placed with its back to the rest of the space can also mark out a zone.

If the room really is tight, push the sofa against the wall and float the smaller pieces instead.

## 4. Layout Idea: The Long, Narrow Living Room

A narrow room is easy to get wrong because everything ends up in a line.

- **Split it into two zones.** Seating at one end, and a desk, dining spot or storage at the other.
- **Use a rug to define the seating.** It stops the room reading as a corridor.
- **Keep one side clear** for the walkway.
- **Put the TV on the short wall** so the seating faces the length of the room.

## 5. Layout Idea: The Squarish One-Bedroom Living Room

A squarer room gives you more options, and the trap is placing everything around the edge.

- **Angle the seating** into an L-shape around a rug, with the TV on one wall.
- **Use the corners** for tall, narrow storage or a reading chair.
- **Place a slim console behind the sofa** as a drop zone, desk or extra storage.

## 6. Layout Idea: Open-Plan Condo With a Kitchen

In open-plan condos, the trick is to separate the zones without building anything.

- **Use the sofa back** as a soft boundary between living and dining space.
- **Change the flooring feel** with a rug under each zone.
- **Match the lighting to the zone.** A floor lamp for seating and a pendant over the dining spot.
- **Keep the sight line to the window clear,** because it makes the whole space feel bigger.

## 7. Work Around Baseboard Heaters and Big Windows

Many Canadian apartments have baseboard heaters under the windows, which makes furniture placement less flexible.

- **Keep furniture and long curtains clear of baseboard heaters** and follow the manufacturer's clearance guidance. Blocked heaters are inefficient and can be a safety hazard.
- **Choose low, leggy furniture** for the window wall, or leave the wall clear.
- **Hang curtains so they stop above the heater** or use a curtain style that doesn't hang over it.

If you're not sure, check with your landlord or building manager.

## 8. The Bedroom Layout: Bed, Closet and Walkway

The bed takes up most of the floor in a small bedroom, so its position decides everything else.

- **Put the bed against the wall opposite the door** if you can, which keeps the entry clear.
- **Leave space on at least one side** to make the bed and get in.
- **Check the closet door swing** before you place the bed. A sliding or bi-fold door saves floor space.
- **Use the space under the bed** with low storage boxes.

## 9. Fit in a Work Corner

If you work from home, a desk needs a permanent spot rather than the sofa or the dining table.

- **A slim desk against a wall** in the living room or bedroom is enough for a laptop.
- **A fold-down or drop-leaf desk** disappears when you're not using it.
- **Use a screen or a bookshelf** to separate the desk from the bed if it's in the bedroom.

## 10. Make the Entrance Work

A small entry sets the tone for the whole apartment.

- **Add a slim shoe rack or bench** to stop shoes spreading into the living space.
- **Use wall hooks** (renter-safe adhesive ones or over-door hooks) for coats and bags.
- **Keep the path from the door clear.** Nothing should block the entrance, even in winter when boots and coats pile up.

## Tips for Moving Into a Small Apartment

- **Measure doorways, hallways, stairwells and the elevator** before you buy a large piece of furniture. A sofa that doesn't fit through the door is an expensive mistake.
- **Book the freight or service elevator** in your building if you're moving furniture in, and ask about any move-in rules.
- **Choose pieces that come apart** or arrive flat-packed if access is tight.
- **Buy furniture in stages.** Live with the space for a couple of weeks and add pieces as you see what you actually need.

Retailers such as IKEA, Structube and Wayfair Canada sell apartment-size furniture, and Facebook Marketplace is a good place to find second-hand pieces, but always check the exact dimensions against your tape layout first.

## Common Layout Mistakes to Avoid

- **Buying furniture before measuring.** Measure the room and the way in.
- **Pushing everything against the walls,** which leaves an awkward empty middle.
- **Blocking the main walkway,** which makes the whole room feel small.
- **Ignoring heaters, doors and windows.** These decide what fits where.
- **Buying too many small pieces.** A few well-chosen pieces beat lots of little ones.

## Final Thoughts

A good small apartment layout is about proportion and flow: furniture scaled to the room, a clear main walkway and zones that make each part of the space feel like it has a job. Measure first, test with tape, and change the layout before you change anything else.
