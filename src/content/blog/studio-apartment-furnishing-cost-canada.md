Moving into an empty studio apartment is exciting until you realize that the space is completely bare and everything from a mattress to a spatula needs to be acquired. Whether you are a student arriving for your first semester, a newcomer to Canada setting up your first home, or someone starting fresh after a major life change, knowing what to expect in terms of furnishing costs can help you budget realistically and avoid both overspending and uncomfortable shortcuts.

This guide breaks down the cost of furnishing a studio apartment across four major Canadian cities: Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and Calgary — including what Facebook Marketplace furniture actually sells for in each city, since secondhand Marketplace listings are where most budget-conscious Canadians fill in the gaps. All prices are in Canadian dollars and reflect 2026 market conditions, drawing from current retail pricing at popular stores and typical secondhand market listings.

## Why Costs Vary by City

Furnishing costs in Canada are not uniform. Two factors create significant city-to-city differences:

**Retail pricing** for major chains like IKEA is largely standardized across the country. A KALLAX shelf costs the same in Montreal as it does in Calgary. However, smaller retailers, local furniture stores, and delivery fees vary. Delivery from IKEA in Vancouver can run $79 to $149 depending on order size, while in Calgary it may be less due to a closer distribution centre.

**Used furniture pricing** varies enormously. Toronto and Vancouver have the most expensive secondhand markets in the country. A used IKEA MALM dresser that sells for $60 to $80 in Calgary might fetch $100 to $150 in Toronto. Montreal sits in between, with a generally more affordable used market, partly due to the July 1 moving day tradition when enormous quantities of furniture appear on curbs and online listings simultaneously.

## Category-by-Category Breakdown

### Sleeping: Mattress and Bed Frame

The mattress is the single most important purchase for your studio. This is not the place to cut corners to the absolute minimum.

| Item | Budget Option | Mid-Range Option | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mattress (double/full) | $200 - $350 | $500 - $900 | IKEA, Endy, Douglas, Casper |
| Bed frame (double/full) | $100 - $200 | $250 - $500 | IKEA, Structube, Wayfair |
| Sheets and pillow set | $40 - $80 | $80 - $150 | Walmart, HomeSense, Simons |

**Used mattress note:** Buying a used mattress is generally not recommended due to hygiene and bed bug risks, which are a real concern in Canadian apartment buildings, particularly in Toronto and Montreal. Used bed frames, however, are excellent value. Facebook Marketplace and Kijiji regularly list IKEA bed frames in good condition for 40 to 60 percent of retail price.

**City-specific pricing for used bed frames:**

|---|---|

## Where to Shop: Store Guide

### New Furniture

**IKEA** remains the default for Canadian apartment furnishing. The best value is in storage solutions, bed frames, and kitchen items.

**Structube** is a Canadian chain with a slightly more polished aesthetic than IKEA at moderate prices. Stronger in sofas, dining tables, and accent pieces. Delivery times can be long (4 to 12 weeks for popular items).

**EQ3** is a Canadian furniture company based in Winnipeg offering well-designed, mid-range furniture. More expensive than IKEA or Structube but significantly better build quality, especially for sofas.

**Wayfair.ca** is useful for specific items, especially shelving, small tables, and lighting. Prices are competitive but quality is inconsistent. Read reviews carefully.

**Walmart Canada** is underrated for apartment basics: kitchen items, bedding, towels, basic shelving, and lighting.

### Used Furniture

**Facebook Marketplace** is the dominant platform for used furniture in all four Canadian cities. Best time to shop: end of month (people moving out) and September (students leaving).

**Kijiji** is still active, particularly in Ontario and the prairies.

**Value Village** and other thrift stores carry furniture, kitchen items, and decor at very low prices.

**Montreal's July 1 moving day:** If you are in Montreal and need furniture, the days surrounding July 1 are extraordinary. Enormous quantities of furniture appear at fire-sale prices.

## Tips for Each Budget Tier

### Bare Minimum ($500 to $800)

Buy your mattress new for hygiene reasons and get everything else used. Prioritize: mattress, bed frame, basic kitchen kit, one set of sheets and towels, a couple of lamps.

### Comfortable ($1,500 to $2,500)

Buy your mattress, a new sofa or sofa bed, and kitchen essentials new. Source your bed frame, dining table, storage, and accent items used.

### Styled ($3,000 to $5,000)

At this level, you can furnish entirely new from stores like IKEA and Structube, with a few EQ3 or Article pieces as focal points.

---

Resist the urge to furnish everything at once. Move in with the essentials (mattress, basic kitchen kit, a lamp, towels) and live in the space for a week or two before making bigger purchases. You will quickly discover what you actually need versus what you thought you needed.

Furnishing a studio apartment in Canada is entirely achievable on a modest budget. The combination of IKEA's standardized pricing, a robust secondhand market, and a few strategic Dollarama runs can have you comfortably settled for well under $1,500 in any city.
