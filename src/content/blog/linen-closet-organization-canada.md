A linen closet in a small Canadian apartment is typically 60–90 cm wide, 40–50 cm deep, and 180–200 cm tall, with 3–4 fixed shelves. Most of them are full but barely functional — sheets fall out when you pull one set, towels are stacked too high to retrieve without everything shifting, and the bottom shelf holds a mix of things that have no other home.

The fix takes about 30 minutes and costs very little.

---

## Step 1: Edit the Contents

Remove everything. Sort into three piles:

**Keep:** Bedding and towels in regular rotation, cleaning supplies that belong here, medicines if this is the storage spot.

**Relocate:** Items that ended up here by default but belong elsewhere — tools, batteries, random small items, cleaning products for specific rooms.

**Discard:** Single pillowcases with no match, worn towels (downgrade to cleaning rags or discard), extra blankets that haven't been used in years, expired medicines.

Most people discard or relocate 20–30% of linen closet contents in this step. That freed space is more useful than any organizer.

---

## Step 2: Shelf Assignment by Category

Assign each shelf a single category. Mixed shelves — where towels share space with cleaning supplies and random items — mean nothing has a clear home and the closet returns to chaos within weeks.

**Top shelf (hardest to reach, 175–200 cm):**
Extra blankets, duvet inserts stored in their bags, seasonal items, rarely-used guest bedding. Keep in labelled bags or bins — items at this height are stored, not accessed regularly.

**Upper-middle shelf (eye level, ~140–165 cm):**
Bedding sets. One set per bed, stored as a unit using the pillowcase method (see FAQ above). If you have two beds, one half of the shelf per bed. This is the most-accessed shelf — it should have the clearest organisation.

**Lower-middle shelf (~100–130 cm):**
Towels. Stack towel sets together (bath towel + hand towel + face cloth) rather than all bath towels together then all hand towels. Retrieving a full set should be one motion. Roll towels instead of folding flat if the shelf is deep — rolled towels use less shelf depth and let you see everything without unstacking.

**Bottom shelf (floor level or lowest shelf):**
Cleaning supplies, extra toilet paper, medicines if stored here, or an overflow bin. Heavy items belong at the bottom — a full bottle of floor cleaner on a top shelf is a risk.

## Step 3: Add Storage Without Replacing Shelves

Most apartment linen closets have fixed shelves with 40–50 cm of vertical space between them — much more than a folded towel or sheet set needs. This gap is wasted unless you add a half-shelf organizer.

### Half-shelf organizer (stackable shelf insert)

A half-shelf insert sits on the existing shelf and creates a second level in the space above. A folded sheet set is typically 15–20 cm tall. A 40 cm gap between shelves means 20 cm of dead air above every stack. A half-shelf insert fills that gap.

**Cost:** $15–$30 at Amazon.ca. Expandable versions adjust to different shelf widths.

### Clear bins and baskets

Group small items in bins so they do not scatter when you retrieve something nearby. A small bin for face cloths, a bin for pillowcases, a bin for cleaning supplies — each bin pulls out as a unit.

**Cost:** $5–$12 per bin at Amazon.ca, HomeSense, or Dollarama.

### Labels

A label on each bin or zone eliminates the "just put it wherever" habit that undoes all organisation within a week. Peel-and-stick labels from a label maker (~$20–$30 at Canadian Tire for a basic Dymo model, labels included) or handwritten labels on masking tape are equally effective.

### Door back organizer

The back of the linen closet door (if hinged) holds an over-door organizer with pockets or hooks. Use it for:
- Spare medicine bottles
- Small cleaning tools (a lint roller, a stain remover stick)
- Extra toiletries
- Rarely-used but must-be-accessible items

**Cost:** $15–$30 at Amazon.ca.

## The Pillowcase Sheet-Folding Method

Fitted sheets are notoriously difficult to fold neatly. The pillowcase storage method solves this:

1. Lay the fitted sheet flat and tuck one corner into the other, forming a rough square
2. Fold in thirds to make a rectangle
3. Fold the flat sheet in thirds lengthwise, then into a rectangle matching the fitted sheet size
4. Stack: flat sheet, fitted sheet, then fold pillowcases into neat rectangles and place on top
5. Slide the whole stack inside one pillowcase
6. The pillowcase holds the complete set — place it on the shelf as one unit

Grabbing a full sheet change becomes one motion: take the pillowcase bundle, pull out the contents at the bed. Takes 3 minutes to fold a set once you have done it a few times.

---

## Towel Rolling vs. Folding

**Folded flat towels** stack efficiently on a deep shelf and look neat, but the stack height means you can only see the top towel — retrieving from the middle destabilises the pile.

**Rolled towels** stand vertically in a row on the shelf, letting you see every towel and retrieve any one without disturbing others. They use slightly more horizontal space per towel but significantly less vertical space — a 40 cm high shelf fits two rows of rolled towels (using ~30 cm of the height) vs. one neat stack of flat towels.

For a small linen closet, rolling works better on any shelf where you have multiple towels and want clear access without unstacking.
