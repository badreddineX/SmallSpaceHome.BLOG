Facebook Marketplace is where Canadian apartments get furnished. A studio in Toronto, a one-bedroom in Vancouver, a first place in Montreal — the majority of people setting up a small space on a budget end up on Marketplace at some point. Furniture that cost $800 new sells for $150 used, and in a city where rent already takes most of the budget, that difference matters.

The problem is that Marketplace is uneven. The same platform that lists a nearly-new IKEA KALLAX for $40 also lists a mystery-smell sofa for $200 and a glass table with a hairline crack the photos don't show. Knowing what to search for, what to inspect, and what to walk away from makes the difference between a furnished apartment and a money-losing frustration.

## What Facebook Marketplace Furniture Actually Costs in Canada

Before shopping, knowing the going rate prevents overpaying and helps you recognize a genuine deal.

**Sofas (2–3 seat):**
- Older fabric sofas in average condition: $50–$150
- Clean fabric sofa, good condition, major brand: $150–$350
- Leather or faux-leather sofa, good condition: $200–$500
- Nearly-new sectional from IKEA/Article/Structube: $400–$900

**Bed frames (queen):**
- Basic metal frame: $30–$80
- IKEA wood frame (MALM, HEMNES, BRIMNES), good condition: $80–$200
- Solid wood or upholstered frame: $150–$400

**Dressers:**
- IKEA MALM or HEMNES 6-drawer, good condition: $60–$150
- Solid wood vintage dresser: $80–$250
- Cheap pressboard dresser with damage: worth $0 — skip it

**Dining tables (4-person):**
- IKEA LISABO or MELLTORP, good condition: $40–$100
- Solid wood dining table: $80–$300
- Glass dining table, no chips: $50–$150

**Bookshelves:**
- IKEA BILLY in white, good condition: $20–$60
- Solid wood bookcase: $50–$200

**Desk:**
- IKEA MICKE or LINNMON setup: $30–$80
- Solid wood desk: $60–$200

These ranges reflect 2026 listings in major Canadian cities. Montreal runs 10–20% lower than Toronto or Vancouver on average. Smaller cities (Ottawa, Halifax, London) vary — supply is lower, so good pieces go fast but asking prices can be higher relative to condition.

## The Best Categories on Facebook Marketplace Canada

### Solid wood furniture — best value

Solid wood ages well and holds up to apartment moves. A solid oak dresser from 1990 in good condition is a better buy than a flat-pack dresser from 2022 with swollen bottoms. Search specifically for:

- **Solid oak** or **solid maple** dining tables — refinish the top if scratched, still lasts 20 more years
- **Mid-century modern** pieces — high demand in cities, but solid construction justifies the price
- **Vintage dressers** — dovetail joints in the drawers mean solid wood construction; staple joints mean MDF

How to tell: look at the edges and the back. Solid wood has visible grain on edges; MDF or particleboard has a flat compressed edge. The back panel of a solid wood piece is often thin wood (okay) or thin plywood (fine); MDF or cardboard-thin backing indicates lower construction quality throughout.

### IKEA pieces — easiest to evaluate

IKEA furniture is the most predictable buy on Marketplace. You know the exact original price, the dimensions are published on IKEA's website, replacement parts are available, and assembly instructions are free online. A KALLAX shelf unit in good condition is worth buying at 40–60% of new price. A MALM dresser with all hardware is worth buying at 50% of new.

What to check on IKEA pieces: look for swollen particleboard on the bottom (indicates water exposure), check drawer runners for cracks, and confirm all original hardware is included (missing cam nuts or screws add a trip to IKEA).

### Glass tables and mirrors — often underpriced

Glass items photograph well and ship poorly, which makes sellers price them low to avoid the hassle of storing them. A glass dining table with a chrome or wood base often sells for $50–$100 on Marketplace when the equivalent at Structube is $400+. Inspect for chips on edges (run your finger around the entire perimeter — chips you can feel are fine, chips you cannot see until you look from an angle are fine; chips that catch on fabric or skin are not).

### What to avoid

**Upholstered items with unknown history**: A fabric sofa from a stranger's home is a risk — pet dander, odours, and stains that have been cleaned but not eliminated. If you buy an upholstered sofa, check every cushion, smell the back and sides (not just the seat), and press firmly on all cushions to check foam degradation. A sofa that smells like air freshener has been freshened for a reason.

**Mattresses (except new-in-bag)**: Never buy a used mattress. The sanitary issues are not fixable. A new mattress at Costco (Sealy or Simmons queen, ~$300–$500 CAD) is a better use of money than a used mattress at any price.

**Pressboard furniture with water damage**: Swollen edges, delaminated surfaces, or soft spots in particleboard are permanent. The piece will continue to deteriorate and is not worth moving even for free.

**Items listed as "priced to move" with no explanation**: Usually means there is a reason it has not moved. Ask directly: "Why are you selling it?" A normal answer ("moving," "upgrading," "doesn't fit our new place") is a green light. Vague or no answer warrants extra inspection.

## How to Search Effectively

### Search terms that find furniture faster

Facebook Marketplace search is exact-match heavy — searching "sofa" will not show listings titled "couch" or "loveseat." Search multiple terms separately:

- Sofa + couch + loveseat + sectional
- Dresser + chest of drawers + bureau
- Bookcase + bookshelf + shelving unit + shelves
- Bed frame + bed + bedframe + queen bed
- Desk + work desk + office desk + writing desk

**Filter by distance first**: Set radius to 10–15 km before browsing. Furniture that requires renting a van for a 45-minute drive needs to be a significantly better deal to justify the cost and effort.

**Sort by "Date Listed: Newest"** rather than relevance. New listings move fast; browsing by relevance shows you old listings that have already been picked over.

**Set a price alert**: Facebook does not have native price alerts, but searching your item daily with the "newest" filter takes 2 minutes and surfaces new listings before they sell.

### How to filter for quality in photos

Good listing photos show:
- Multiple angles including the back and underside
- Close-ups of any damage explicitly mentioned
- The item in a real home (not a blank wall with a flash — that hides texture and colour)

Red flag photos:
- Single photo, no details
- Heavily filtered or colour-adjusted
- Photos that don't show the seat cushions, drawer fronts, or glass edges

Message sellers of poorly-photographed items asking for specific shots before committing to a viewing trip.

## Inspecting Furniture Before You Pay

Always see the item in person before paying. This is normal and expected — no legitimate furniture seller will refuse an in-person inspection.

### The full inspection checklist

**Structural:**
- Sit on sofas and chairs — no sag, no creaking, no wobble
- Open and close every drawer — should slide smoothly, no binding, no missing slides
- Check table and desk legs for wobble (put pressure on all four corners)
- Check bed frame joints for cracks or loose hardware

**Surface:**
- Inspect wood surfaces in direct light at an angle — scratches that look minor straight-on can be deep
- Run fingers over glass edges (chips, roughness)
- Check fabric for stains under direct light — look at the arms and seat cushions specifically
- Smell upholstered items before agreeing to take them

**Hardware:**
- Confirm all screws, bolts, and hardware are present (important for IKEA pieces)
- Check drawer handles and pulls — replaceable, but worth noting
- Check any mechanisms (recliner, extending table, sofa bed) actually work

**For anything with a base and glass top**: Press down on the glass and check the mounting points — glass tops that are just resting on rubber pads with no clip or bolt are a tip risk.

### What to negotiate after inspection

If the item has minor damage that was not in the listing (a scratch, a small stain, missing hardware): mention it specifically and offer 20–30% less than the asking price. Most sellers will meet in the middle. If the damage is more than cosmetic — a wobbly leg, a broken drawer slide, a crack in the frame — walk away unless the price drops enough to justify the repair cost.

## Logistics: Getting Furniture Home

### What you can move with a car

A hatchback or SUV handles:
- Dining chairs (disassembled flat)
- Small dressers (4-drawer MALM fits in most SUVs with rear seats folded)
- Flat-pack items
- Coffee tables and side tables
- Mirrors (cushioned with a moving blanket)

### Renting a van or truck

**Home Depot Tool Rental** (Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary, Edmonton): Pickup truck rental from ~$19 for the first 90 minutes + km. Requires a valid driver's licence and a credit card hold.

**U-Haul cargo van**: Available in most Canadian cities, ~$19.95 base + $0.69/km + fuel. Book online same-day for most locations. A cargo van fits a queen mattress, sofa, and 3–4 boxes in one trip.

**TaskRabbit movers**: Available in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, Calgary, Ottawa, and Edmonton. Book a single mover with a van for 1–2 hours — typically $60–$120 for a local furniture pickup. Use this when you do not drive or do not want to deal with a rental.

### Elevator booking in apartment buildings

Most Canadian condo and apartment buildings require you to book the elevator for moves. A single furniture pickup counts — contact your building management or superintendent 24–48 hours in advance to book the service elevator. This is free in most buildings. Showing up without a booking and using the passenger elevator with a sofa creates real problems with neighbours and building management.
