# First Apartment Checklist & Moving-In Guide
### The Renter-Safe Guide to Setting Up Your First Canadian Apartment

*Built for smallspacehome.ca*

## How to Use This Guide

Moving into your first apartment is exciting — and overwhelming. This guide covers exactly what you need, what you can skip, how to set it all up without damaging a wall you don't own, and what your landlord can and can't legally charge you in your province. Work through it room by room, check items off as you go, and use the budget tiers to spend smart, not just cheap.

**A note on prices and product names:** every item lists a category first (what to look for) and a specific example second. Retailers discontinue and rename products constantly — treat the named products as a starting reference, not a shopping list to follow blindly.

## PAGE 4 — Renter-Safe Setup Guide (No-Drill Solutions)

*This is the part generic checklists don't cover — decorate the place properly without leaving a single hole to argue about at move-out.*

☐ **Command Strips/Hooks** — hang art, mirrors, and light decor without nails ($10–15 for a variety pack, Canadian Tire/Amazon.ca)

☐ **Tension rods** — for curtains, closet dividers, or under-sink organization. No drilling into window frames ($10–20, IKEA/Amazon.ca)

☐ **Peel-and-stick backsplash** — removable vinyl tiles for kitchen/bathroom, fully reversible ($25–40 for a full backsplash, Amazon.ca)

☐ **Freestanding shelving** — instead of wall-mounted shelves, use a ladder shelf or bookcase that needs zero wall damage ($40–80, IKEA)

☐ **Removable wallpaper** — for one accent wall, fully peelable at move-out ($30–50/roll, Amazon.ca)

☐ **Rug pads instead of nailed-down flooring fixes** — solves slipping without altering the floor ($15–20)

☐ **LED puck lights (stick-on)** — extra lighting in closets/under cabinets, no wiring or drilling ($20–25, Amazon.ca)

## PAGE 5 — Move-In Inspection Checklist (Protect Yourself at Move-Out)

*Do this on day one, before you unpack a single box.*

**Why this matters more than you think:** at move-out, disputes come down to one question — was that mark already there? Without dated proof, it's your word against your landlord's, and you're the one who loses. Ten minutes on day one is the cheapest insurance in this entire guide.

☐ Photograph every room from multiple angles — timestamp matters, so text the photos to yourself or use an app that timestamps automatically

☐ Walk through and note every existing mark: scuffs, scratches, stains, chips — write them down room by room

☐ Test every outlet with your phone charger

☐ Test all taps (hot + cold) and check under sinks for leaks

☐ Test the stove, oven, and fridge

☐ Check smoke detector and carbon monoxide detector — test the button, note the battery date

☐ Check all windows and doors open, close, and lock properly

☐ Note the state of blinds/curtains if included

☐ Send your notes + photos to your landlord/property manager in writing (email, not just text) within 48 hours of move-in

☐ Keep a copy for yourself — this is your proof at move-out

☐ **BC and Alberta only:** your landlord must complete a written condition inspection report with you at move-in. Do it, read it before signing, and keep your copy — in BC, a landlord who skips it can lose the right to claim against your deposit.

## PAGE 6 — Know Your Deposit Rights (By Province)

*Most "first apartment checklists" are written for a US audience and tell you to protect your "security deposit." In much of Canada, that deposit isn't legal in the first place — and knowing this can save you a month's rent before you even move in.*

| Province | What a landlord CAN collect | What they CANNOT collect |
|---|---|---|
| **Ontario** | Last month's rent deposit (max 1 month) + a refundable key deposit equal to actual replacement cost | **Damage, security, and pet deposits are all illegal.** Your last month's rent deposit cannot be used for damage or cleaning |
| **Quebec** | First month's rent only | **Security deposits are prohibited** under art. 1904 of the Civil Code. A landlord cannot require any amount other than rent |
| **British Columbia** | Security deposit up to ½ month's rent, plus a pet damage deposit up to ½ month's rent (1 month combined max) | More than the combined 1-month cap. Deposit must be returned within 15 days of move-out or you may be owed double |
| **Alberta** | Security deposit up to 1 month's rent, held in an interest-bearing trust account | An increase to the deposit mid-tenancy. Landlord has 10 business days after move-out to return it or itemize deductions |

**☐ If you're in Ontario or Quebec and a landlord asks for a "damage deposit" — that request is not legal.** You can refuse. If you already paid, Ontario tenants can apply to the Landlord and Tenant Board (LTB) and Quebec tenants to the Tribunal administratif du logement (TAL) to get it back.

**☐ Everywhere in Canada:** even where no deposit exists, a landlord can still pursue you for genuine damage through the tribunal. That's exactly why the Page 5 photo record matters — it's your evidence either way.

*This is general information current as of 2026, not legal advice. Rules change and vary by province — confirm with your provincial tenancy board before acting on a dispute.*
