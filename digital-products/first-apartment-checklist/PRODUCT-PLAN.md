# Canada — Digital Product Plan

## The Product: First Apartment Checklist & Moving-In Guide

**Who it's for:** First-time renters moving into a small apartment in a Canadian city (Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal) — the exact reader already on smallspacehome.ca.

**What it is:** A ~12 page PDF/Canva download with:
1. Room-by-room essentials checklist (kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, living room) — checkboxes, not paragraphs
2. **Renter-Safe Setup page** — no-drill solutions (Command strips, tension rods, peel-and-stick backsplash) — the real differentiator vs. generic checklists
3. **Move-in inspection checklist** — dated photo/damage record that protects you from move-out damage claims
4. **Deposit rights by province (ON/QC/BC/AB)** — what a landlord can and cannot legally collect. This is the hardest page for a competitor to copy and the strongest proof the guide is genuinely Canadian
5. Budget tiers per item (budget/mid/splurge) — CAD pricing, real Canadian retailers named (IKEA Canada, Amazon.ca, Wayfair Canada, HomeSense)

**⚠️ Framing correction (2026-07-21):** earlier drafts sold this as protecting your "damage deposit." **Damage deposits are illegal in Ontario and prohibited in Quebec** — two of the three named target cities (Toronto, Montreal). Only BC caps a real security deposit (½ month) and Alberta allows up to 1 month. Selling a "protect your deposit" guide to an Ontario renter destroys the Canadian-authenticity claim that is this product's entire moat. The fix turned the liability into the moat: the product now teaches deposit *rights per province*, which no US-authored competitor can replicate. Verified against Ontario RTA (CLEO), Civil Code of Québec art. 1904 (Éducaloi), BC RTA (gov.bc.ca), Alberta RTA (alberta.ca).

## Competition Reality Check

**Not low-competition** — Etsy's "new home checklist" category has 5,000+ listings, "new home moving checklist" 1,000+. Less saturated than budget spreadsheets (32K–59K) but still real, contested. What the 2,300-review listing proves: a specific, well-differentiated listing can break out inside competition — not that the niche is uncontested.

**Differentiation that matters** (ranked by how hard it is to copy):
1. **Provincial deposit rights (ON/QC/BC/AB)** — hardest to copy, requires actual Canadian legal knowledge, and instantly signals the guide isn't a reskinned US template. This is the lead differentiator now, not a bonus page.
2. **Renter-specific setup** (no-drill, damage-claim-safe) — addresses pain points generic checklists don't
3. **CAD prices at real Canadian retailers** — easiest to copy but still a real gap; most competing content is US-focused per the blog's own competitor research

## Audience Reality Check (added 2026-07-21)

**The blog audience and the product audience are not the same people, and the plan must not assume they are.**

- The product serves someone **about to move in**.
- The blog attracts someone who **already lives** in a small apartment and wants decor/organization ideas.

The 4.9★/2,300-review Etsy listing proves **Etsy search demand** from people actively moving. It does not prove that Pinterest decor browsers convert on a moving-in guide. Those are different funnels, and the earlier plan quietly used one as evidence for the other.

**What this changes:**
- **Etsy is the primary revenue channel for cold search traffic** (people actively searching Etsy with move-in intent) — but that's a separate listing, never linked from the blog. **Fourthwall is the only destination for warm blog/Pinterest traffic**, full stop.
- Blog CTAs are a bonus, not the thesis — which is why the CTA rollout below is now phased instead of shipped across all 23 posts at once.
- The free lead magnet exists to convert the blog's *actual* audience (already-moved-in renters who still want the deposit-rights info) into an email list, which is worth more at current traffic than a $12 sale that won't happen.

**Why the blog only links to Fourthwall:** the blog is our own asset; every dollar of blog-driven traffic should land on our own storefront, not hand the sale — and a platform cut — to a marketplace we don't control. Etsy and Gumroad exist to catch buyers who were never going to read the blog in the first place; they don't need a blog link to find the product. **Every `[PRODUCT LINK]` placeholder in every CTA file in `Article CTAs/` resolves to the Fourthwall listing, with no exceptions.**

## Seasonality — This Demand Is Not Flat

Canadian first-apartment demand spikes twice a year, hard. The earlier plan treated it as year-round, which would have meant launching into the trough.

| Window | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **July 1 — Quebec "Moving Day"** | Most Quebec leases legally turn over on the same date. A province-wide, single-day moving event with no equivalent anywhere else in North America. Montreal is a named target city. | The single biggest ownable hook this product has. Pinterest pins + blog content live by **mid-May**; product listed and promoted **June 1–30**. |
| **Sept 1 — student lease wave** | University/college leases start nationwide; first-apartment intent peaks for the 18–24 segment. | Second push, **early-to-mid August**. Angle the copy at students/first movers. |
| **Oct–Apr** | Trough. | Build, refresh, list-build. Don't launch here. |

**Content implication:** the blog currently has no Moving Day or student-move content at all. Two posts targeting those windows would feed the product far better than any CTA added to an existing decor post.

## Sequencing

**Do not build yet.** Per the blog's own roadmap, digital products are Phase 4 — after Pinterest traffic is actually flowing (posting started 2026-07-20). Building now, before any visitor exists to test it on, is premature.

**Revised order of operations:**

1. **Now (free, no build risk):** ship the free lead magnet (Pages 5–6) and an email capture on the 3 Priority-1 posts. Costs nothing, validates demand, and builds the list you'll launch to.
2. **Validate:** watch lead-magnet opt-in rate. A blog that can't give the free version away won't sell the $12 one — that's the real pre-sell signal, better than gauging DMs.
3. **Build:** design the full PDF in Canva only after step 2 shows real opt-ins, timed to land **before the May–June Moving Day window**.
4. **Launch:** Fourthwall listing goes live first and is the only link ever placed in a blog CTA or Pinterest bio. Etsy listing is a separate, parallel listing for cold search traffic — it never gets a blog link.
5. **Expand CTAs:** roll out beyond the Priority-1 posts only once something actually converts.

### Rollout is phased — do NOT ship all 23 at once

As of 2026-07-21 the site sits at ~172 GSC impressions and average position ~37. It is effectively pre-traffic. Adding 42 commercial CTA boxes across 23 posts converts approximately nobody, while stacking a heavy monetization signal onto a thin, four-day-old domain that is still earning Google's trust. That is downside with no upside.

| Phase | Trigger | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase A (now)** | — | The 3 Priority-1 posts only, and pointing at the **free lead magnet**, not a paid product that doesn't exist yet |
| **Phase B** | Lead magnet converting at a measurable rate + product built | Same 3 posts switch to the paid CTA; add the 4 strongest Priority-2 posts |
| **Phase C** | A post reaches page 1 / real traffic, and Phase B converts | Remaining Priority-2 posts |
| **Never** | — | The 2 Priority-3 seasonal posts get a footer line at most |

### Rules for placement (apply to every article rolled out)

1. **One CTA box mid-article** (after the reader has gotten real value from the post — not before), **one at the end.**
2. **Never a popup, never a sitewide banner** regardless of which article someone lands on.
3. Priority 1 articles get the most visually prominent CTA treatment (larger box, maybe an image); Priority 2 gets a standard text CTA; Priority 3 stays light or omitted.
4. The 23 per-article CTA files in `Article CTAs/` are **written and ready**, not scheduled — treat them as a library to draw from per phase, not a to-do list to ship.
